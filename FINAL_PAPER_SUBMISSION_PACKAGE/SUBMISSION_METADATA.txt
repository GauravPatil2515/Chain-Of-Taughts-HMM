TITLE:
CoT-HMM: Structural Plausibility Scoring of Chain-of-Thought Reasoning via Hidden Markov Models

CoT-HMM: Structural Plausibility Scoring via HMMs

ABSTRACT (Word count: ~180 words, 1260 chars):
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting elicits step-by-step reasoning from large language models (LLMs), yet verifying whether generated reasoning is structurally sound remains challenging. We propose CoT-HMM, an unsupervised, one-class structural plausibility filter that abstracts reasoning traces into sequences of eight semantic tags and trains a multi-resolution Ensemble of Hidden Markov Models (M=12) on correctness-verified positive traces—requiring no human step-level annotations of incorrect reasoning—to model latent structural transition dynamics. Operating at ~150 ms per sample on standard CPU, CoT-HMM targets a distinct niche from heavy neural process reward models: structural anomaly detection rather than fine-grained token-level verification. Through controlled structural error injection on real LLM-generated traces (Qwen2.5-0.5B on GSM8K, N=39 verified correct traces, B=10,000 percentile bootstrap resamples), we demonstrate that CoT-HMM reliably detects structural failures: premature conclusions (AUC = 0.990 [95% CI: 0.966, 1.000]), circular reasoning loops (AUC = 0.761 [95% CI: 0.645, 0.866]), and missing computation steps (AUC = 0.658 [95% CI: 0.531, 0.778]). Critically, a semantic-only corruption condition yields an AUC of 0.501, confirming that the method isolates structural rather than semantic plausibility—providing a complementary, highly efficient first-stage screening filter for composite verification pipelines.
